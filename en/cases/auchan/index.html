Add-to-order: letting customers change an order after payment

An add-to-active-order scenario for Auchan's website, mobile web and app

About the project

Auchan is an international hypermarket chain and one of the largest grocery retailers in Russia. For the chain, online is no longer a side channel but a significant share of the whole business: the auchan.ru website, the My AUCHAN app, delivery from hypermarkets, click-and-collect and express delivery.

Context

Auchan is one of Russia's largest grocery retailers, with an online store and mobile apps. Online grocery ordering is rigid: the customer builds a cart, picks a delivery slot and pays, and then the order goes to the store for picking.

Between payment and the start of picking there is a window when the order already exists, but nothing is physically happening to it yet. That's exactly when customers most often remember they forgot the milk.

How it used to work

placed an order → remembered an item → canceled the order and rebuilt it, placed a second order with separate delivery, or contacted support

Business challenge

The task came from the business – from Auchan's product managers and the head of the department. It had three sources:

01

Support

Regular "please add an item to my order" requests that operators couldn't resolve

02

Cancellations and re-orders

Customers canceled paid orders and rebuilt them just to add a couple of forgotten items

03

Lost revenue

Forgotten items were bought elsewhere or not bought at all

BeforeAfter
An order can't be changed after paymentAn order can be added to until picking starts
Forgot an item → cancel and reorderForgot an item → add it to the same order and the same delivery slot
The issue is resolved via supportThe issue is resolved independently in a minute
The "Thank you for your order" page is a dead endThe "Thank you for your order" page is an upsell point

The main goal

let customers add items to an active order on their own, on any platform – and in doing so raise the average order value, reduce cancellations and take load off support.

How it was

Goals and metrics

Business goalProduct metric
More revenue per orderAverage order value, share of orders with an add-on
Fewer lost ordersShare of cancellations before picking starts
Fewer "catch-up" ordersShare of repeat orders within 2 hours of the first one

Counter-metrics: order picking time, share of payment errors on recalculation, main cart conversion. Add-to-order must not make worse what already worked.

Objectives

  • Design a new user journey for adding items to an existing order
  • Show all the screens and states needed to understand the feature
  • Adapt the solution for the website, mobile web and the mobile app
  • Stay within Auchan's existing brand book and UI kit

Constraints

Add-to-order is limited not by the interface but by operations: the warehouse, pickers, couriers and the payment flow. We received these constraints from the business and development teams, and they shaped almost every design decision.

ConstraintWhat it meant for the interface
Items can only be added before picking startsThe scenario is tied to the "Awaiting picking" status and must end gracefully when the order moves to "Picking"
No more than 10 items per add-onAn always-visible "N / 10 added" counter is needed
Weight limit: the whole order can't exceed 65 kgThe condition has to be explained before selection starts, not as an error at the end
The delivery slot and address don't changeThe add-on doesn't go through checkout again
Two payment methods with different mechanicsThe final step differs for online payment and payment on delivery
Not every item can be addedA "This item can't be added to your order" state that suggests a similar item
Auchan's brand book and UI kit can't be changedNew elements are built from existing components and patterns

Key decision: add-to-order as a store mode

The main question was where the customer picks the items to add.

OptionProsWhy we didn't choose it
A separate screen with a curated selection of productsQuick to buildThe customer is limited to recommendations and can't find what they forgot
The regular cart with an "add to order" labelA familiar patternMixes a new order with the add-on; it's unclear where the item will go
Add-to-order mode across the entire catalogThe full assortment, familiar product cards and searchChosen: harder to build, but doesn't limit the customer

Hypothesis

if the add-to-order module behaves like a cart and works in every section of the store, customers don't have to learn a new scenario – and they'll add what they actually forgot, not what we pushed on them.

User flow

In Figma, the flow is built separately for each of the three entry points and each platform: 9 branches in total, each with the catalog, adding items, three exception pop-ups, the add-on page and the updated order.

#1. Entry points

Add-to-order should meet customers where they remember the forgotten item – not where it's convenient for us to place it.

We identified three such moments and added an entry point to each. Everywhere, the conditions are worded the same way and shown before the click: how many items can be added, the weight limit and the deadline.

Thank you for your order

The hottest moment: the customer has just paid and still has their shopping list in mind. Next to the order information we added a "Forgot something?" block: an informer with the conditions, a recommendations slider based on the items in the order, and a link to the catalog. The screen in the mobile app is shorter, so only the informer and the "Go to catalog" button remained there.

My orders

Customers come back here later, once they remember an item. Order cards with the "Awaiting picking" status got an "Add to order" button. On desktop it opens a pop-up with recommendations for quick adding; on mobile it goes straight to the catalog.

Order details page

The third entry point is for those who came to check the order status. The button sits in the "Total" block next to the amount, because adding items changes that amount.

Trade-off

The recommendations pop-up exists only on desktop. On mobile it took up the whole screen and duplicated the catalog, so there we kept a single direct path.

#2. The add-to-order module

A constant companion in the catalog that answers three questions: which order am I adding to, how much more can I add, and how much does it cost.

From any entry point, the customer lands on the top level of the catalog with add-to-order mode already on, and then browses sections as usual. On desktop, the module is pinned as a floating bar in the corner of the screen; in mobile web and the app, it's built in above the navigation bar, where the cart usually lives.

What the module shows

  • Order number and status
  • "N / 10" item counter
  • Add-on total
  • "Add to order" action

Why

  • Customers don't confuse the add-on with a new cart
  • The limit is visible before you hit it
  • No surprises in the total at the final step
  • You can finish from any page

Product cards work the same way as in the regular catalog: "Add to cart", then a quantity stepper. Only the destination changes: the item goes into the add-on instead of the cart.

If an item can't be added right now, the customer sees a "This item can't be added to your order" pop-up right in the catalog. Instead of a dead end, the pop-up suggests a similar item that can be added straight away, and nothing already selected is lost.

#3. Confirmation and payment

Before confirming, the customer sees the original order and the added items on one screen.

The "Order add-on" page follows the cart's logic: at the top, the original order with its delivery slot; below, the added items, where you can change quantities, remove an item or clear the whole add-on; on the right, a summary with the weight and the add-on total. The "Back" button returns the customer to the catalog page they were on, so they can throw in something else.

The hardest part is the money. The mechanics depend on how the original order was paid.

Payment methodWhat happensWhat the customer sees
On deliveryThe order total is recalculatedThe new total in the order; payment on delivery as usual
OnlineThe updated total is pre-authorized; after picking, a refund is issued and the actual cost is chargedA payment page with a clear amount, then the updated order

The main risk

with online payment, the customer sees two transactions for a single order in their banking app. That's why we show the add-on amount and the order total separately at every step, so the charge doesn't look like a mistake.

After confirming, the customer returns to "My orders" and sees the same order with the added items and the new total.

#4. Exits and edge cases

The add-on lives inside someone else's process and can end without the customer choosing to end it.

SituationSolution
The customer changed their mindExit via a button with a "Stop adding items?" confirmation. If they cancel, the customer keeps choosing; if they confirm, the items are removed and they return to "My orders"
The order moved to "Picking" while the customer was choosingA "Can't add to order" pop-up ("Time to add items has run out" on mobile). The customer is offered to move the selected items to the cart and place a new order
The customer came to add to an order that's already being pickedThere's no add-to-order button; a new order is suggested instead
An item can't be added to the orderA "This item can't be added to your order" pop-up with a similar item; selection continues

Moving items to the cart when the status changes is a small detail that saves the purchase: the customer doesn't lose what they've selected and doesn't leave frustrated.

When picking has already started, the pop-up explains why and immediately offers two ways out: "Move to cart" for a new order, or "Exit add-to-order".

Three platforms – one logic

The scenario was designed for desktop, mobile web and the app at the same time. The logic, copy and states are shared; only the way the module fits into navigation differs.

ElementDesktopMobile Web / App
Add-to-order moduleFloating bar in the corner of the screenBlock above the navigation bar
Quick addPop-up with recommendationsDirect link to the catalog
Exit confirmationModal windowBottom sheet
"Thank you for your order"Informer, recommendations slider, link to the catalogMobile web – same as desktop; app – only the informer and the link to the catalog

Under the brief, the solution had to fit within Auchan's existing brand book and UI kit. The only new components were the ones specific to the scenario: the add-to-order module, the recommendations modal and the three exception pop-ups. Everything else was built from existing cards, buttons and informers.

Validation and expected results

How we validated the solution

Before development, the scenario went through internal UX reviews with product managers and developers, plus testing on a clickable prototype. I don't have access to post-launch quantitative results: Auchan's team ran the measurements after the mockups were handed off. Below is the validation plan I built into the solution.

After launchWhich entry point works best and where customers drop offEvent funnel: entry → add → review → confirm

Stakeholders and development

Final decisions were made by Auchan's product managers and head of department, while the design expertise sat with the studio. That meant arguing every decision in terms of the business task, not taste.

  • Presented options with their pros and implementation cost, rather than one "right" design
  • Discussed every warehouse and payment constraint with developers first, and only then designed
  • Designed edge cases together with the main scenario, not after it

Process: brief from product managers → scenario and constraints → options → review → mockups for three platforms → handoff → design support during development

The handoff included the module's states, the copy for the conditions, rules for each order status and annotations for the payment branches.

What I would do differently

01

Metrics from the start

I would agree with the client's team upfront on access to post-launch results, so I could see the solution's impact and not just the mockups.

02

Test earlier

I would test the main risk – confusing the add-on with a new cart – on a prototype before detailing all three platforms.

03

A wider add-to-order window

I would work with the operations team on adding items during picking, for products from store zones the picker has already passed.
